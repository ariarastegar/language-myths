When people say 'oldest', they usually mean one of two things. One is 'least changed', or conservative. The other is 'written down earliest', or attestation depth. Both can be measured, but neither tells you how old a language is.

Conservatism is always patchy. A language can keep an archaic noun system while its sounds or vocabulary have changed enormously.

## Caveats

- **Conservatism is real.** Lithuanian keeps a lot of old Indo-European noun morphology, and that is a legitimate fact. It just isn't a fact about age.
- **Isolates feel old.** Basque has no demonstrated relatives, which only means its family tree can't be traced. It doesn't mean it is older.

## Examples

- **Icelandic:** modern readers can manage the medieval sagas in writing, but pronunciation has shifted a great deal since then.
- **Attestation:** Sumerian and Egyptian are written from around 3000 BCE, and Hittite's Anitta text (around 1700 BCE) is the oldest known Indo-European text. Those are the record's oldest pages, not the oldest languages.
