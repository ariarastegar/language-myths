Proto-Indo-European is reconstructed by comparing its descendants. Sanskrit is one of those descendants, and a very well documented and conservative one. That made it vital for the reconstruction, but not a source for the other languages.

## Caveats

- **Sanskrit's importance is real.** William Jones's famous 1786 remark that Sanskrit, Greek and Latin must share a common source helped launch comparative linguistics.
- **It isn't the earliest written either.** Hittite is written down from around 1700 BCE, the oldest attested Indo-European language. The Vedic hymns, though, were composed and passed down orally long before anyone wrote them.
- **Jones wasn't first.** Others had noticed the resemblance before 1786. His remark got famous because of his reputation.

## Examples

- Sanskrit *pitár-*, Greek *patḗr*, Latin *pater*, English *father*: these are cousins descended from one reconstructed form, not one taken from another.
