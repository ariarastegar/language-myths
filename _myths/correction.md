---
claim: "Children learn to talk because adults correct their mistakes."
one_liner: "Children get little consistent grammatical correction and mostly ignore what they get. They learn from exposure and use."
topic: "Learning"
order: 12
---

Classic studies of parents and children found that parents mostly correct whether what the child said is true, not whether the grammar is right. Children also over-regularize (*goed*, *mouses*), producing forms they never heard, because they are working out the rules themselves.

## Caveats

- **Implicit feedback exists.** Adults often recast what a child says in correct form, and that may help. The debate is how much it matters.
- **How much is innate is contested.** This entry says nothing about whether grammar is innate. That's a separate, live debate.

## Examples

- A widely quoted exchange reported by David McNeill (1966) has a child repeat 'nobody don't like me' after every correction, until finally saying 'nobody don't likes me'.
- Children's own errors show the gap: in one exchange a child says 'I thought they were all womans' and the adult replies 'They're not all women'. That's a recast, not a correction.
