Every generation's innovations look like decline to the generation before. What they actually do is change the language, and today's 'correct' forms were once someone's sloppy innovations.

Informal writing like texting is an extra register. Research hasn't found that it wipes out the ability to write formally.

## Caveats

- **Language loss is real, just elsewhere.** Minority languages are dying when communities switch to dominant languages. That's a real crisis, but it isn't English getting worse.
- **Individual skills vary.** A person can lack command of a formal register. That's about education and exposure, not about the language degrading.

## Examples

- **Literally** has been used figuratively in print since at least 1769, including by Dickens ('literally feasted his eyes') and Twain ('literally rolling in wealth').
- In the 1490s, the printer Caxton complained that English varied so much from county to county that people couldn't even agree on the word for 'eggs'.
