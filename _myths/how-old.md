---
claim: "How old is English? Is Tamil older than Malayalam?"
one_liner: "Language is the substance, not the container. You can ask how old a river is, but not how old the water in it is."
topic: "History & change"
order: 1
---

Every living language is passed on unbroken from one generation to the next, back past any record. So in terms of substance, all of them are equally old.

What can be dated is the container: a name, an identity, a polity, a written standard. Asking how old the idea of 'England', or people calling themselves 'English', is has a real answer, even though the speech beneath it kept changing.

Labels like Old, Middle and Modern English mostly divide up the written record. The spoken language underneath never had a point where one stage died and the next took over.

## Caveats

- **Some languages really do have birthdays.** Nicaraguan Sign Language arose among deaf children in the 1970s and 80s, creoles form in identifiable periods, and Esperanto dates to 1887. The rule holds only where transmission was continuous.
- **Period boundaries aren't arbitrary.** They cluster around real upheavals such as the Norman Conquest, or the arrival of printing and standardization. They are just not deaths.
- **The question often means something else.** Usually it is really asking about attestation depth or about conservatism (see those entries).

## Examples

- **Franks → French:** the name carries on with no cutoff, but Frankish, a Germanic language, dies. The Romance lineage of French had to exist already before it could take the name.
- **Tamil vs Malayalam:** 'Tamil' is an older identity and literary tradition. Malayalam's separate identity emerges later, but its speech is no 'younger'.
- **Greek, Turk:** these are old self-identifications. Their age is a fact about one ethnonym's history, not about how old the speech is.
