Meaning is how people use a word now. Etymology explains how it got here, and words regularly drift far from where they started. Treating the origin as the 'true' meaning is called the etymological fallacy.

## Caveats

- **Etymology is great for history.** It tells you about contact, culture and sound change. The mistake is only using it to decide what a word should mean today.
- **Some contexts look backward on purpose.** Legal or religious interpretation may care about original meanings. That's a choice about interpretation, not a fact about language.

## Examples

- **Nice** comes from Latin *nescius*, 'ignorant'.
- **Decimate** once meant 'kill one in ten'. Insisting on that today is a classic case of the fallacy.
