Languages of small, non-literate or technologically simple societies are just as complex as any others. Where English is simple, as in its inflections, other languages are complicated, and the reverse is true too.

When someone says a language 'has no grammar', it usually means it has little inflection, or that no one has written a grammar book for it.

## Caveats

- **Complexity does vary.** Linguists no longer insist all languages are equally complex overall. Some research suggests languages of small, close-knit communities build up more morphological complexity, while heavy adult language-learning tends to simplify it.
- **Creoles are a live debate.** Whether creoles are systematically simpler is contested. That is a claim about specific subsystems, not a claim that they are primitive.

## Examples

- **Archi** (Dagestan) is famous for allowing an enormous number of forms for a single verb.
- **English** has very little inflection but a lot of complexity in word order, phrasal verbs and idiom.
