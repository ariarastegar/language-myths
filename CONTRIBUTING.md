# Contributing

Thank you for helping. The myths on this site change rarely; what makes it useful is the evidence attached to each one. You can contribute without knowing anything about GitHub.

## Add evidence

Open a myth on the site and choose **Add evidence**, or go to *Issues → New issue → Add evidence*. You'll be asked for:

- **The myth** it relates to
- **What it does to the answer:** supports it, complicates it, or is a counterexample. Evidence that complicates an answer is just as welcome as evidence that supports it.
- **Type:** study, primary text, data or corpus, reference work, or news and popular writing
- **Citation and link**
- **Languages** it concerns
- **What it shows**, in one to three sentences of your own words
- Whether you've **read the source itself**, not only a summary or abstract

A maintainer reviews every submission. Once it's accepted, it appears on the site with your GitHub username.

## Propose a myth

Use *Issues → New issue → Propose a myth*. Write the claim the way people actually say it, then a short answer and a longer explanation. Include at least one academic study (a peer-reviewed article or scholarly book) whose findings answer it: myths are only published once such a study is attached, and the short answer should summarize what the research finds.

## Check existing evidence

Many entries say "Not yet checked against the original". They were added from abstracts or summaries. If you can read the original, open an issue saying what you found, whether it confirms the summary or corrects it.

## Discuss

To question an answer or a piece of evidence, open an issue whose title starts with the myth's ID in brackets, for example `[latin-died]`. The **Discussion** button on each myth lists these threads.

## Guidelines

- Summarize in your own words, and don't paste long passages from sources.
- Prefer original sources (papers, primary texts, datasets) to news coverage or encyclopedias.
- One piece of evidence per submission.
