There is no structural test that sorts varieties into 'languages' and 'dialects'. The label follows states, standards, writing systems and identity.

For linguists, everyone speaks a dialect, standard varieties included.

## Caveats

- **Mutual intelligibility is a real criterion.** But it is gradual, it can be lopsided, and it often contradicts the official labels.
- **Distance can be measured.** Linguists distinguish languages separated by structural distance from those separated by deliberate standardization (Kloss's 'Abstand' and 'Ausbau').
- **Weinreich didn't coin the quip.** In 1945 he reported hearing it from a member of his audience at a YIVO lecture.

## Examples

- **Scandinavian:** Norwegian, Swedish and Danish are 'languages' that are largely mutually intelligible.
- **Chinese:** Cantonese and Mandarin are called 'dialects' but are not mutually intelligible when spoken.
- **Hindi–Urdu** and the former **Serbo-Croatian**: one spoken system and several official languages.
