Feeling that a language is 'logical' usually just means it is regular in the places you happen to be looking, or that it carries prestige. Every language is regular in some areas and irregular in others.

## Caveats

- **Regularity can be measured.** One language really can have fewer irregular verbs than another. That's a fact about one subsystem.
- **Logical languages can be designed.** Lojban was built around predicate logic. Its unnaturalness for everyday use rather proves the point.

## Examples

- **Double negation:** it is standard in Spanish (*no vi nada*) and Russian, and stigmatized in English (*I didn't see nothing*). Neither is any more logical.
- **Evidentiality:** Turkish verbs mark whether you witnessed something or only heard about it, which English leaves unsaid. Does that make English illogical?
